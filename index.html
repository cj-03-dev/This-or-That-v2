    The data

    Where the numbers come from

    Method

    How the score works

    Every score is per 100 g, uses fixed rules, and lists every point it adds or takes away.

    Longevity at the core

    The longevity score follows the Alternative Healthy Eating Index, the eating pattern most strongly linked to healthy ageing in a study of 105,015 people followed for up to 30 years. Each food starts from its food type, then gains points for fibre, protein, long-chain omega-3, healthier fats and potassium, and loses points for free sugar, saturated fat, salt and industrial trans fat.

    Tessier et al., Nature Medicine, 2025

    4 goals, one core

    Weight loss, protein and pre-workout scores are half longevity and half the goal itself, so a food never wins a goal while being poor for long-term health. Weight loss rewards fewer calories and more protein and fibre per calorie. Protein rewards protein per 100 g and per calorie, and complete protein. Pre-workout rewards quick carbs with little fat and fibre.

    Made for your age and sex

    Your daily needs come from the official reference values for your age and sex. Nutrients that matter more for you, such as iron for women under 51, or vitamin D and B12 after 50, earn extra points, and salt counts more from age 51.

    NASEM Dietary Reference Intakes, NIH Office of Dietary Supplements; Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2020 to 2025; PROT-AGE 2013

    Fair to dairy and fats

    The evidence on saturated fat is mixed. Cutting it lowered heart events by about 17% in trials, with no clear effect on deaths. So milk, yogurt and cheese carry no saturated fat penalty, butter, ghee and cream carry half, and only the part above 10% of a food's calories counts.

    Hooper et al., Cochrane Review, 2020

    How processed it is

    Each food is placed in a NOVA processing group from 1 (fresh or minimally processed) to 4 (ultra-processed), based on how its source describes it. Group 4 loses 6 points.

    Monteiro et al., NOVA classification; Lane et al., BMJ, 2024

    Why not a single-food index?

    Food Compass scores single foods and was linked to lower risk of death, but it needs details official tables do not hold, such as additives and specific processing steps. So the site keeps the diet pattern with the strongest evidence at its core and borrows only what official data can support.

    Mozaffarian et al., Nature Food, 2021; O'Hearn et al., Nature Communications, 2022
